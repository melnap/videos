Number fifteen. Salad greens and other watery vegetables. Lettuce, cucumbers, celery for snacking, and raw tomatoes are mostly water. When that water freezes, it breaks the plant cells, and they thaw limp and soggy. Chopped celery or tomatoes are fine for a soup pot. For a crisp salad, they're ruined. Don't fill freezer space with them. And vegetables you freeze yourself, like green beans or broccoli, need a quick blanch in boiling water first, or they come out dull and tough.

Number sixteen. Unopened cans. A can in the freezer is a mistake people make when they're trying to save leftovers or cool a drink fast. The food expands as it freezes, which can bend the can and break the seal along the seams. If you want to freeze the rest of a can of beans, tomato sauce or broth, move it into a freezer container first.

Part four. Thaw it safely and protect it.

Number seventeen. Thaw tomorrow's dinner tonight. The safest way to thaw is in the refrigerator, and it takes planning. Move tomorrow's meat from the freezer to a plate on the bottom shelf of the refrigerator tonight. A big turkey needs much longer. The USDA says about twenty-four hours for every four to five pounds, which is four full days for a twenty-pound bird. If you forget, thaw it in its sealed bag in cold water and change the water every thirty minutes. Never leave meat on the counter to thaw. The outside warms up long before the middle does.

Number eighteen. The refreezing rule. People throw away a lot of good food over this. If meat thawed in the refrigerator and you changed your plans, you can put it back in the freezer without cooking it, according to the USDA. It may lose a little texture, but it's safe. Food you thawed in cold water or in the microwave is different. Cook it first, and then you can freeze the cooked dish.

Number nineteen. The coin in a cup. This is the one I promised you. Fill a small cup with water and freeze it solid. Then lay a coin on top of the ice, and keep the cup in your freezer all winter. If you're away during a storm, or the power goes out at night, look at that coin when you get back. If it's still on top, the freezer stayed frozen. If it sank to the middle or the bottom, everything in there thawed at some point and then froze again, even if it looks frozen now. It costs nothing, and it answers a question you can't answer any other way.

And number twenty. When the power goes out, keep the door shut. Every time the door opens, the cold air you need pours out. A full freezer gives you about forty-eight hours, and a half-full one about twenty-four, but only if the door stays closed. Once the power is back, check your thermometer from number one. The USDA says food that still has ice crystals, or that stayed at forty degrees or below, can be refrozen. And any food you're unsure about gets thrown away. Don't taste it to find out.
