People are told cream cheese doesn't freeze, and for spreading on a bagel, that's true. The texture changes. But the NDSU guide says cream cheese can be frozen for later use in cooking, in dips or as icing.

So if you bought the big pack for cheesecake and dip, freeze the extra blocks in their foil wrappers inside a freezer bag. Let them thaw in the refrigerator and use them in anything baked or mixed.

Number eleven. Hard cheese.

A holiday cheese board always leaves leftovers. The NDSU guide says hard and semi-hard cheese can be frozen in pieces of no more than half a pound, sealed tightly. After thawing, it will be a little crumbly and won't slice neatly, but the flavor stays good.

That makes frozen cheese perfect for anything cooked. Macaroni and cheese, casseroles, soups. A Washington State University Extension handout gives hard cheeses like cheddar and Swiss about six months in the freezer.

Number ten. Milk.

Yes, milk freezes. The NDSU guide says pasteurized milk can be frozen, including low-fat and nonfat. The key is room to expand. Pour off a little from the top of the jug before it goes in, or the jug can split.

When it thaws, the milk may look a little separated. Shake it or stir it and it comes back. Thaw it in the refrigerator, use it soon, and don't refreeze it. It's a good way to save the half gallon that would otherwise go bad while you're traveling for the holidays.

Number nine. Buttermilk.

Most recipes need one cup of buttermilk, and the carton holds four. The NDSU guide groups buttermilk with the cultured dairy foods that turn a little grainy and may separate when frozen. But they still work perfectly in cooking.

So freeze the rest in one-cup portions, labeled. Next time you make biscuits, pancakes or cornbread, you won't have to buy another carton.

Number eight. Bread and rolls.

The University of Missouri Extension guide says baked bread and rolls keep about three months in the freezer. To bring them back, thaw them, wrap them in foil and warm them in a three-hundred-degree oven for about fifteen minutes. Rolls take five to ten.

That means you can bake or buy your dinner rolls two weeks before Thanksgiving, freeze them, and have one less thing to do on the busiest day of the year.

Number seven. Cookie dough.

The same Missouri guide gives cookie dough about three months in the freezer. Roll it into logs or scoop it into balls on a tray, flash freeze them on the tray until solid, then bag them. Logs of refrigerator-cookie dough can be sliced and baked without thawing. Bulk dough should thaw in the refrigerator until you can drop it by the spoonful.

Make your Christmas cookie dough in November, on a quiet weekend, and bake it fresh in December.

Number six. Pie crust.

Pie crust is the step that makes holiday baking feel like work. The Missouri guide says unbaked pie crust keeps six to eight weeks in the freezer, and you can bake it without thawing. A baked crust keeps two to three months.

So make three or four crusts at once, freeze them flat or in the pans, and the hardest part of Thanksgiving pie is done before the week even starts.

Number five. Unbaked fruit pies.

This one surprises people most. The Missouri guide gives unbaked fruit pies about eight months in the freezer. You bake them straight from frozen, and you cut the vents in the top crust after freezing, not before.
