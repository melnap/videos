Your holiday leftovers don't belong in the trash. Most of them belong in your freezer.

Two cups of gravy nobody finished. A week later it all goes in the trash, along with the money you paid for it, and the time it took to make.

That isn't carelessness. Most of us learned that the freezer is for meat, ice cream and frozen peas. Nobody told us about the rest.

So today I'm sharing fifteen holiday foods you can freeze now, how to do each one right, and how long each one keeps, so your holiday cooking costs less and wastes less. One of them goes into the freezer straight from the bag, no washing. One only freezes well if it has enough fat in it. Before we start, one rule that applies to everything on this list. The government's FoodSafety.gov chart says food kept frozen at zero degrees stays safe indefinitely. The storage times I'll give you are for quality, meaning flavor and texture. So a few weeks past isn't a reason to panic. It's a reason to use it soon. And the biggest threat to quality for every item on this list is freezer burn, the dry, leathery damage that happens when air reaches the food. Press the air out of bags, wrap things tightly, and use rigid containers with lids that seal. That one habit protects everything.

For the details, I'm using guides from North Dakota State University Extension, the University of Missouri Extension, Penn State Extension and the National Center for Home Food Preservation.

Number fifteen. Fresh herbs.

Thanksgiving recipes call for a few sprigs of sage, thyme or rosemary, and the store sells you a whole package. The rest wilts in the refrigerator.

The NDSU guide says to wash the herbs, drain them, pat them dry, wrap a few sprigs in freezer wrap and seal them in a container. One honest note. Frozen herbs go limp when they thaw, so they won't work as a garnish. But in stuffing, soup or gravy, you won't know the difference.

Number fourteen. Cranberries.

This is the one that goes into the freezer straight from the bag. Fresh cranberries are in stores for only a few months a year, and they're often on sale around Thanksgiving.

Penn State Extension says you can put the bags of fresh cranberries directly into the freezer for up to twelve months and use them without thawing. Just rinse them under cold water before you cook. So buy a few extra bags now, and you'll have cranberry sauce in March and cranberry muffins in June.

Number thirteen. Heavy cream.

This is the one that only freezes well if it has enough fat. The NDSU guide says to freeze only heavy cream with forty percent butterfat or more. Lighter cream and half-and-half don't freeze well.

There's one catch. Frozen cream won't whip up to its full volume. So use it in soups, sauces and mashed potatoes. And here's a trick from the same guide. If you have whipped cream left over from pie, sweeten it, spoon it in little mounds onto a tray, freeze them, and then bag them. They're perfect on hot cocoa. That trick is called flash freezing, freezing pieces separately on a tray so they don't clump, and it works for a lot of things on this list.
