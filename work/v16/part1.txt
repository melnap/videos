First, where this list comes from. FEMA's preparedness site, Ready.gov, tells families to build a disaster supply kit with at least several days of non-perishable food and one gallon of water per person per day. FEMA also published a longer guide with the American Red Cross and the USDA called Food and Water in an Emergency. That guide goes further. It suggests a two-week supply and explains how long each kind of food keeps. Most of what I'll share comes from those two sources.

And because winter storms don't always clear up in three days, I'll also show you how one of the most prepared communities in the country handles the question of how much is enough.

Number fifteen. Instant coffee, tea and cocoa.

This one surprises people, but it's on FEMA's list of foods that keep for a very long time in proper containers. And in winter it matters more than you'd think. When the house is cold and the power is out, a hot drink is comfort, warmth and routine all at once. Ready.gov even lists comfort foods as part of a good emergency supply, because a calm family makes better decisions. Keep the jars tightly closed and dry.

Number fourteen. Salt.

This is the cheap one that keeps for years. FEMA's guide lists salt among the foods that can be stored indefinitely in proper conditions. It seasons plain rice, beans and canned vegetables, so the same few foods don't become impossible to eat by day five. Keep the container sealed so it doesn't pick up moisture and harden.

Number thirteen. Bouillon.

FEMA's guide lists bouillon products with the long keepers. A cube or a spoonful of powder in hot water turns into a warm cup of broth, or a pot of rice into something with flavor. Just keep it dry, because moisture turns the powder into a solid lump.

Number twelve. Dry cereal and granola.

Ready.gov lists dry cereal and granola because they need no cooking at all. They're breakfast for children, a snack for adults, and filling straight from the box. FEMA's guide says ready-to-eat cereals keep about a year when they're stored in a sealed metal container rather than an open box. Once a bag is open, fold it shut and put the whole thing in a container with a lid.
