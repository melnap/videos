Number six. Frozen vegetables.

Remember the USDA forecast? Fresh vegetables are expected to rise close to seven percent this year. In winter, fresh produce also travels farther, and it spoils faster than we'd like. Costco's big bags of frozen broccoli, green beans and mixed vegetables let you skip all of that.

Frozen vegetables are picked and frozen at their peak. They keep several months in the freezer. The NDSU guide puts it at up to eight. The one rule is to keep them frozen solid. If a bag has thawed and refrozen into one big lump, the quality has already suffered. And freezer burn becomes a real problem when air gets to the surface. Press the air out of the bag before you seal it every single time, and you'll keep freezer burn to a minimum.

Number five. Frozen berries.

A five-pound bag of Kirkland frozen blueberries was about twelve to sixteen dollars at many warehouses this year. That's roughly two and a half to three dollars a pound, for berries that are already washed and ready.

Here's why that matters. Fresh berries last two to three days in the refrigerator. Frozen berries keep eight to twelve months in the freezer. In January, a handful in your oatmeal costs you pennies, and nothing goes to waste.

The mistake is letting the bag sit open on the counter while you make breakfast. Take out what you need, press the air out, seal it, and put it right back. Pressing out that air also helps keep freezer burn away. Don't refreeze berries that have fully thawed.

Number four. Nuts.

Walnuts, pecans and almonds come in big bags at Costco, right when holiday baking starts. But here's the surprise. Nuts are full of oil, and that oil goes rancid. The NDSU guide gives most packaged nuts only about three months unopened in the pantry. Once they're open, they keep four to six months in the refrigerator. For longer storage, freeze them.

So that big bag on your pantry shelf is quietly running against a clock. Mine goes straight into the freezer in a few smaller bags. Frozen nuts don't need thawing for baking. And if a nut ever tastes bitter or smells like old paint, it has turned. Throw that bag away.

Number three. Chicken breasts.

Costco's ten-pound bag of thin-sliced chicken breasts, individually wrapped, was about thirty dollars at many warehouses this year. That's around three dollars a pound. The national average for boneless chicken breast was a little over four dollars a pound earlier this year.

Because each piece is wrapped on its own, you only take out what you need, which makes this one of the best bulk buys for meal prep. Batch a few pieces on Sunday and your lunches for the week are done. Frozen chicken pieces keep about nine months in the freezer. The mistake is thawing them on the counter. Move tomorrow's dinner from the freezer to the refrigerator tonight. It's safer, and the chicken stays juicier.

Number two. Coffee.

Coffee has been one of the most expensive items in the store. The national average for ground coffee was nine dollars and thirty cents a pound in August. Costco's three-pound bag of Kirkland Colombian whole bean coffee was about twenty-five dollars regularly, and around twenty-one on sale earlier this month. That's roughly seven to eight dollars a pound.

But three pounds is a lot of coffee. Once a bag is open, coffee keeps its flavor for only about two weeks in the pantry and about a month in the refrigerator. In the freezer, it keeps six to twelve months. So I divide a new bag into jars that each last about a week. One jar stays out, and the rest go in the freezer. Every cup tastes like the bag was just opened.

And number one. Butter.

This is the one you probably keep in the refrigerator, and it belongs in the freezer.

This summer, Costco's four-pound box of Kirkland butter was under ten dollars at many warehouses. That's around two and a half dollars a pound. The national average in August was about four dollars a pound. For a family that bakes through the holidays, that difference is real money.

But butter only keeps one to two months in the refrigerator. Four pounds is more than most homes can finish in that time. In the freezer, butter keeps six to nine months. So I keep one pound in the refrigerator and the rest in the freezer, wrapped tightly so it doesn't pick up freezer smells. When one pound runs out, the next one moves down. That's it. That one habit turns the best deal in the store into a deal you actually get to keep.
