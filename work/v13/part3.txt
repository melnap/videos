The first is the giant bottle of olive oil. Costco's three-liter bottle of extra virgin olive oil is a good price per ounce. But once it's open, the NDSU guide gives cooking oils about three to five months in the pantry. If you use a tablespoon a day, three liters lasts over six months. The last part of that bottle will be stale or rancid before you reach it. Buy the one-liter size, keep it in a cool, dark cupboard, and buy again when it's empty.

The second is the big containers of ground spices and spice blends. Ground spices lose their flavor after two to three years, and blends after about six months. A container that size takes most families years to finish. You end up seasoning food with dust that only smells like cinnamon. Buy whole spices if you want to buy in bulk, or buy the small jars.

The third is the giant packs of fresh produce. That huge clamshell of fresh berries lasts two to three days. A big box of salad greens lasts three to five, and about two once it's opened. Unless you're feeding a crowd this week, buy the frozen version, which you already saw on my list.

The fourth is the pre-portioned five-pack of ground beef. I wanted this one to be a deal. But at around thirty-four dollars for five pounds, it works out to about six eighty a pound. The national average for ground beef in September was seven dollars and six cents. That's only about a quarter less per pound. And ground beef only keeps three to four months in the freezer, so you can't stock up for long. Compare the unit price with your grocery store's sales before you put it in your cart.

And the fifth is the five-dozen flat of eggs. This is the one in almost every cart. But egg prices have been falling all year as flocks recover from the avian flu outbreaks that drove the egg price crisis over the past couple of years. Farm prices for eggs this summer were far below a year earlier. Eggs in the shell keep three to five weeks in the refrigerator. Buying five dozen ahead in a falling market only gives you a refrigerator full of eggs racing the calendar. Buy what you'll use this month.

So here's the whole idea in one sentence. Buy big only when the food keeps longer than it takes you to use it. Rice, flour, sugar and canned chicken keep for many months on the shelf. Butter, coffee, nuts, chicken and berries keep for months in the freezer. The giant oil bottle, the spice tub, the fresh produce and the eggs don't keep long enough to pay off.

If you take one thing home from your next Costco trip, make it a roll of freezer bags and a marker. Write the date on everything, and always pull the oldest item forward when you put something new away. That is the pantry organization principle called first in, first out, and it is the single habit that prevents waste. A little attention to pantry organization is what turns a big cart into a full pantry that lasts until spring.
