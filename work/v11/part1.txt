Every year, from early November until Christmas, butter is one of the foods stores fight over. Thanksgiving is on November twenty-sixth this year, and holiday baking follows it right into December. Stores know that if they put butter, flour and sugar on sale, families come in for them and fill the rest of the cart while they're there. So butter often turns into what stores call a loss leader, priced low on purpose to get you through the door. Watch the weekly ads starting the first week of November, and you'll usually see it. The sales often run right through Black Friday week, and some stores bring butter back on sale for Christmas baking in December.

So how long does butter really keep? In the refrigerator, about one to two months. In the freezer, about six to nine months for best quality, according to the USDA's FoodKeeper guide and North Dakota State University Extension. And if your freezer stays at zero degrees or colder, it stays safe even longer than that. The six to nine months is about flavor, not safety.

That means butter bought in the middle of November is still at its best in May or even July. One good sale can carry you through the whole winter and well into spring.
